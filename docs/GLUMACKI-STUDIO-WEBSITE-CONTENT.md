# **2\. ABOUT THE STUDIO**

## **About Glumački Studio**

Glumački Studio is a **non-profit acting studio in Belgrade dedicated to the serious study and practice of acting**.

Our goal is to provide actors with a consistent space in which they can train their technique, explore different approaches to performance and develop the practical skills required of a contemporary actor.

We believe that acting is a craft. Like any craft, acting requires practice, discipline, curiosity and a willingness to continually examine and improve the way we work.

Our classes therefore focus on **doing rather than simply discussing acting**. Students work through exercises, scene study, monologues, repetition exercise, script analysis, voice and movement, and other practical approaches to the actor's work. The aim is to give each student concrete tools that they can continue to develop and apply independently.

Our training draws from several acting traditions, with particular emphasis on the work of **Uta Hagen and Sanford Meisner**. Rather than asking every actor to fit into one prescribed method, we explore different tools and approaches and encourage students to understand how and when they can use them effectively.

The studio is open to both **beginners and working actors aged 18 and over**. This creates an interesting and productive dynamic in the room: actors with different levels of experience can train together, challenge one another and contribute different perspectives to the work.

Classes are currently conducted **in English**, making Glumački Studio a space where both international actors living in Belgrade and local actors who want to train in English can work together.

At its core, Glumački Studio combines two things we consider essential to good actor training:

**a high standard of professional practice and an environment in which actors feel comfortable enough to take creative risks.**

# **5\. WHAT WE WORK ON**

## **Practical Actor Training**

Acting is made up of many interconnected skills. Our classes approach the actor's work from multiple directions rather than relying on a single exercise or type of training.

The exact material and exercises may vary from class to class, but our training includes areas such as:

### **Scene Study**

Students work with scenes from real written scripts to explore character, circumstances, relationships, objectives, actions and behavior.

Scene study gives actors the opportunity to apply technique in a practical context. It’s taking the elements we have been working with exercises and theoretical talk, and putting them into the test of the actor’s work. Here is where students learn to investigate what is happening between the characters and how that can be communicated through action and behavior.

### **Monologue Work**

Monologues allow actors to work independently and develop their ability to sustain a character's circumstances, objective and emotional life without relying on another performer.

Students explore text, intention, behavior and the specific circumstances of the character while developing a performance that remains grounded and truthful.

### **Meisner Repetition**

Repetition exercises are used to develop **listening, presence, spontaneity and truthful response**.

The work encourages actors to move away from planning their performance and toward genuinely responding to what is happening in the moment.

### **Script Analysis**

A strong performance begins with a strong understanding of the material.

Students learn to examine the text and read between the lines. With this detective work we can identify the circumstances, relationships, objectives, actions, obstacles and other elements that influence the character's behavior.

The goal is to help actors make informed choices rather than relying solely on instinct or general interpretations of a character.

### **Voice**

The voice is one of the actor's primary instruments.

Classes may explore vocal awareness, clarity, breath, intention and the relationship between voice and emotional or physical action.

### **Movement**

Actors communicate through the entire body, not only through words.

Movement work helps students develop greater awareness of their physical choices, presence, impulses and the relationship between the body and the character.

### **And More**

Acting cannot be reduced to a checklist of exercises.

Classes therefore leave room to explore other aspects of the craft depending on the needs of the group and the material being worked on.

The underlying goal remains consistent:

**to give actors practical tools that make their work more specific, connected and truthful.**

# **6\. OUR APPROACH**

## **Technique Is a Tool — Not a Formula**

Glumački Studio explores multiple approaches to acting, with a particular focus on the work of **Uta Hagen and Sanford Meisner**.

We believe that studying technique should give an actor **more freedom, not more rules**.

Different actors respond to different exercises and approaches. Rather than teaching one rigid formula and expecting every performer to work in exactly the same way, our classes expose students to practical tools and encourage them to understand the principles behind those tools.

### **Uta Hagen**

Hagen's approach places significant emphasis on truthful behavior, circumstances, relationships and the actor's ability to fully engage with the world of the character.

Her work provides actors with practical ways to investigate a scene and make their choices more specific and grounded.

### **Sanford Meisner**

Meisner's work places particular emphasis on **living truthfully under imaginary circumstances**, listening and responding authentically to one's scene partner.

Repetition exercises and related practices help actors develop presence and responsiveness rather than relying on pre-planned emotional or behavioral choices.

### **Our approach in practice**

The purpose of studying these techniques is not to make actors perform "a Meisner scene" or "a Hagen scene."

It is to give actors a broader understanding of the craft and a greater range of tools with which to approach their work.

Students are encouraged to develop their own process — one that is technically informed, personally specific and adaptable to different material and professional situations.

# **10\. COMMUNITY**

## **Serious Training. Supportive Community.**

Glumački Studio takes the work seriously.

We also believe that a serious acting environment does not need to be cold, competitive or intimidating.

Acting is collaborative by nature. You cannot learn to listen without another person to listen to. You cannot learn scene work without a scene partner. You cannot develop your ability to respond truthfully without being willing to take risks in front of other people.

For that reason, community is an important part of the studio — **but it exists alongside the training, not instead of it.**

Our classes bring together people with different experiences, backgrounds and levels of professional development.

A beginner might learn from a working actor's experience. A working actor might discover something unexpected from working with someone who approaches a scene without years of habitual technique.

Students are encouraged to share knowledge, exchange professional tips, support one another and build relationships within the acting community in Belgrade.

The classroom remains a place of focused work.

Outside the exercises and scene work, it can also become a place to meet collaborators, make friends and find people who understand why you keep coming back to acting.

**We train seriously. We work together. We grow together.**
